Interesting Facts & Personal Stories

Moments that couldn't be captured on a phone

From the Journal

This page contains stories, moments I experienced, saw and learned about the Colombian culture which I wasn't able to capture on phone. Some of these moments may have escaped my mind as it took me a really long time to make this website. Here are the ones I still remember.

🌴

Bogotá's Surprising Climate

Colombia and South America in general has a reputation for being a very warm place. While that is true, I was very surprised to find out that the capital of Colombia — Bogotá — is actually a cold place. In the morning it feels like Spring here in Canada, when the weather is transitioning from Winter to Spring. During the day it gets warmer and at night the weather becomes cold again.

02
🌴

A City of Cyclists

I was surprised to see a lot of people biking in the city. The first day I arrived, it was raining and there were still people biking outside.

03
🌴

Colombia, Not Columbia

Colombians do not like it when you spell Colombia as Columbia. A small but important thing to remember before you visit.

04
🌴

A Country Surrounded by Mountains

Everywhere you go in Colombia, you are guaranteed to see a beautiful mountain. This country is surrounded by mountains and that's one of the many things I began to like about Colombia.

05
🌴

Beautiful, Organised Chaos

Colombia is a noisy country and there is a lot of movement in the streets. One YouTuber best used the word chaos to describe this — and I agree. Using this word doesn't mean there is danger or the streets aren't safe. The streets are always busy — businesses trying to draw people in, vendors selling on the streets, people walking to work, music playing, cars honking, motorcycles passing by. Another thing I liked about Colombia.

06
🌴

Colombians Love Music

Colombians love music. The last weekend I was there, there was a place near the hostel and they played music for two days straight, nonstop. It was so loud it felt like I was at the source. On one of our sightseeing trips, we stopped at a small store to buy water and they were playing music so loud that the tour guide had to shout to ask for water.

07
🌴

A Hardworking People

Colombians are hardworking people. Whether they have a job or not, they really earn the money they get. Even those without a job will approach you and put on a performance — singing, rapping, or dancing — for money. Others ask for money and after you give it to them, they give you candy. They are even allowed to board buses for free and perform.

08
🌴

The Relaxed Way of Life

Colombians have a relaxed way of living. While this is a good thing at times, it makes doing things with a Colombian really testing. The person who took pictures of me used his phone. Whenever I asked him to send the pictures, it would take a day or two. He'd say "I will send it to you right now" — and then not do it. I also heard similar stories from other travellers. You have to be patient with them otherwise you will not enjoy your trip.

09
🌴

Women on Motorcycles

Something that took me by surprise was the number of women who drove motorcycles. In Canada you don't see many women on motorcycles, but in Colombia women drive all types. At the place I stayed, a woman next door would come out every morning in a lab coat, hop on her motorcycle and ride to work. It became one of my favourite daily sights.

10
🌴

Beauty on Their Own Terms

For a country that holds beauty to a high standard, it was interesting to see that it's not a big deal for women to let the hair on their arms or legs grow out. I became aware of this at the mall. We were in line to pay and I noticed some nice boots. As I looked up I realised the person was a woman in a skirt — and she had hair on her legs. After seeing so many women the same way, it occurred to me that it is simply not frowned upon there.

11
🌴

The Kindness of Strangers

Colombians are very nice, kind and helpful people. Near the first hostel, I wandered over to watch some guys playing soccer. One of them gestured at me — his teams weren't balanced. We played for an hour and it became one of the highlights of the trip.

At the hostel, an employee named Amelia took an interest in my project. One free day she invited me to her godson's first birthday party. At the end of the party, a little girl about 2–4 years old was going around waving at adults. When she reached me she came closer, put her hands on my thighs and leaned forward. A lady explained in Spanish — "she is trying to give you a kiss on the cheek." In Colombia, female friends and family say goodbye with a kiss on both cheeks. She eventually walked away — but I was the only adult she tried to kiss, and that made me feel like one of them.

12
🌴

The Food Is Incredible

Colombian food is delicious and incredible. I was surprised that I enjoyed about 90% of everything I tried — and I'm a very picky eater. Also, the food is all natural. Unlike in North America where food is altered to grow bigger and last longer, in Colombia most food items go bad after about three days.

Until Next Time

Colombia left a mark on me that no other country has managed to leave before.

Move over Spain — Colombia has taken your spot as my favourite country in the world.
